# DesignLab — Complete Business & Product Definition

DesignLab is a design and creative-services business. Its purpose is to help businesses turn their ideas, products, and brands into clear, usable, professional visual and digital experiences.

It combines UI/UX design, branding, graphic design, motion graphics, and product design. It is intended to operate as a real service business, not merely a portfolio, a content page, or a collection of design experiments.

The following definition combines the decisions and context established for DesignLab with a proposed operating model. Items described as proposed should not be treated as already implemented.

## 1. Identity and positioning

Brand name: DesignLab

A creative and digital design studio.

### Core mission

Help businesses communicate more clearly, present their products more effectively, and deliver better digital experiences through thoughtful design.

### Core positioning

Design that connects visual quality, usability, product requirements, and business objectives.

DesignLab should not compete solely on making attractive graphics. Its value should come from understanding what a business needs to communicate or build, choosing the right design solution, and delivering usable outputs.

For example, a company launching a software product might need a landing page, an interface design, a consistent visual identity, and promotional graphics. DesignLab can coordinate those assets instead of treating them as unrelated design jobs.

## 3. Target customers

DesignLab's natural customers are organizations that need better design but do not want to build a complete internal design team.

|Customer segment|Typical need|
|---|---|
|Startups and founders|Brand identity, MVP interfaces, landing pages, pitch decks|
|Small and medium businesses|Marketing materials, websites, consistent visual identity|
|Technology companies|UI/UX, dashboards, design systems, product improvements|
|Marketing and content agencies|Reliable design production and campaign support|
|Established brands|Brand refreshes, campaigns, presentations, digital experiences|
|Companies launching products|Product visuals, interfaces, launch assets, promotional material|

The best initial customers are those with a clear business problem, a defined decision-maker, and a project that can be delivered within a predictable scope.

DesignLab can serve clients directly and also deliver design work for other Joinly businesses.

## 4. Business model

DesignLab should be capable of generating revenue through several formats, without making every project a custom engagement.

### A. Fixed-scope projects

One-time engagements with specified deliverables, deadlines, revision limits, and prices.

Examples: a brand identity, a landing page, or a pitch deck.

### B. Standardized service packages

Predefined combinations of deliverables with clear boundaries and repeatable workflows.

Examples: a startup launch design package or a set of campaign creatives.

### C. Monthly retainers

Recurring design capacity for companies that need ongoing assets.

Examples: monthly marketing graphics, campaign adaptations, or ongoing product-design support.

### D. Partner and white-label delivery

Provide design services behind the scenes for agencies, developers, and other service providers.

## 6. The integrated learning and production model

An important part of DesignLab's intended development is that university learning, practical design, content creation, and business development reinforce one another.

### Learn

Study design, product management, visual communication, and relevant technical concepts.

### Build

Apply the knowledge to a client problem, a Joinly project, or a clearly scoped portfolio project.

### Document

Record the problem, constraints, decisions, iterations, and final result as a case study.

### Publish

Turn selected parts of the process into a short video, post, or educational resource.

Acquire and improve

Use the portfolio to attract clients, evaluate results, and refine the service.

The aim is to avoid treating studying, building a portfolio, creating content, and finding clients as four separate jobs.

For example, studying interface hierarchy could lead to redesigning a real dashboard, documenting the before-and-after, publishing a short breakdown, and using the case study to approach companies with similar products.

There is one important constraint: content production must not become a more expensive activity than the work it is supposed to support. Video should be produced when the same project can supply most of the underlying material, rather than requiring an entirely separate production process.

## 8. Portfolio and client acquisition

DesignLab needs evidence that it can solve real problems, not merely a gallery of attractive images.

Each portfolio case study should explain:

1. Context: Who the work was for and what the situation was.
2. Problem: What was unclear, ineffective, difficult, or missing.
3. Constraints: Time, budget, technical limitations, and requirements.
4. Approach: The reasoning behind the design decisions.
5. Execution: Key iterations, interface screens, identity assets, or campaign materials.
6. Outcome: What was delivered and, where measured, what changed.
    

For speculative projects, label them honestly as concept work. Do not present hypothetical business outcomes as measured results.

Potential acquisition channels include:

- Direct outreach to founders and business owners.
- Partnerships with developers and marketing agencies.
- Referrals from existing clients and Joinly businesses.
- Publishing case studies and selected design-process content.
- Freelance platforms and relevant professional communities.

The portfolio should support a specific offer. A prospective client should be able to understand what DesignLab does, who it helps, what the deliverables are, and how to start a project.

## 9. Operating model

The initial operating model should be small and standardized rather than a full agency structure with many departments.

|Function|Responsibility|
|---|---|
|Business development|Prospecting, discovery calls, proposals, follow-ups|
|Product and design direction|Requirements, scope, creative direction, quality standards|
|Design production|UI/UX, graphics, branding, motion, according to project scope|
|Delivery and quality assurance|Reviews, revisions, file organization, final handoff|
|Marketing and portfolio|Case studies, selected content, website, credibility|
|Administration|Contracts, invoicing, project tracking, profitability|

These are functions, not necessarily six separate employees. One person can own several functions, and specialists can be brought in for work that exceeds internal capacity.

### Standard delivery process

1. Lead qualification: Confirm the client's problem, budget, decision-maker, and deadline.
2. Discovery and scope: Define the objective, deliverables, constraints, acceptance criteria, and exclusions.
3. Proposal and agreement: Confirm price, schedule, payment terms, and revision limits.
4. Production: Create and review the work in agreed milestones.
5. Client feedback: Collect consolidated feedback and make the agreed revisions.
6. Quality check and handoff: Verify files, formats, links, responsiveness where relevant, and implementation requirements.
7. Closeout: Collect outstanding payment, archive the project, request permission to publish a case study, and evaluate actual delivery cost.

The objective is to make recurring work easier to deliver without imposing unnecessary process on small projects.

## 10. Technology and AI

DesignLab has also been considered as part of Joinly's broader AI and automation work, including the possibility of a DesignLab AI agent.

Potential uses include:

- Turning client briefs into structured requirements.
- Generating creative directions and initial concepts.
- Creating content variations from approved brand assets.
- Automating repetitive resizing and asset preparation.
- Supporting case-study drafts and project documentation.
- Helping with lead research, proposal preparation, and reporting.

These are potential applications, not confirmed deployed capabilities.

AI should reduce production time or improve the quality of the output. It should not become a separate product-development project unless there is a clear reason to invest in it. Client confidentiality, licensing, brand consistency, and human quality control still apply.

## 11. Performance measurement

DesignLab should measure business performance and delivery quality separately from visual popularity.

|Area|Useful measures|
|---|---|
|Acquisition|Qualified leads, discovery calls, proposal conversion|
|Revenue|Revenue, gross margin, recurring revenue|
|Delivery|On-time delivery, actual hours versus estimated hours|
|Quality|Revision rounds, defects, client satisfaction|
|Efficiency|Revenue per delivery hour, reusable assets, production time|
|Portfolio|Published case studies, qualified inquiries attributed to them|
|Retention|Repeat clients, renewal rate, referrals|

A useful initial measure is:

$$E = \frac{\text{Project revenue} - \text{direct delivery costs}}{\text{delivery hours}} $$

This estimates the contribution generated per delivery hour. It should be interpreted alongside project complexity, quality, and client acquisition costs, rather than used as the only measure of performance.

## 12. Current strategic status

The latest strategic context places DesignLab in a constrained-development phase. It is part of the Joinly ecosystem, but its full-scale development has been frozen while other priorities receive attention.

That distinction matters:

- Defined: The broad service domain, its place in Joinly, its intended portfolio role, and the white-and-purple website preference.
- Previously considered: An Academy/Studio structure, an AI agent, and integration with learning and content production.
- Not established by the available context: A complete current team, published price list, validated customer segments, mature delivery operations, or a fully deployed AI agent.

The near-term model should therefore be a lightweight service studio: accept well-scoped projects, standardize repeatable deliverables, create case studies from real work, and seek revenue without spending heavily on infrastructure.

## Formal definition

DesignLab is a design and creative-services business within Joinly that provides UI/UX and digital product design, branding and visual identity, graphic design, motion graphics, and related product-design services. It helps businesses communicate their value, improve digital experiences, and bring products and campaigns to market through coherent, practical design solutions.

Its business model combines fixed-scope projects, standardized service packages, recurring design support, and partner delivery. Its longer-term ecosystem role is to connect practical learning, project execution, portfolio development, and commercial work—while keeping operations lean and using automation where it demonstrably improves delivery efficiency.
